You paid for it. You haven't used it yet.

You paid $12,000 in January. Only $1,000 of it is a January expense.

The example

Take a year of business insurance, paid up front. Expense it all at once and January looks terrible, while February to December look better than they really are.

Instead, it sits on the balance sheet as a prepaid expense, and $1,000 moves to the P&L each month. The same goes for annual software, rent paid in advance and yearly subscriptions.

It's the flip side of deferred revenue: money moves now, but the cost belongs to the months you actually use what you paid for.

The cash leaves once. The cost arrives monthly.
