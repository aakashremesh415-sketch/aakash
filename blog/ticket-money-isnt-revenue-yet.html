Ticket money isn't revenue (yet)

Sold $100,000 of tickets for an event that's still three months away? Great news for the bank balance. But it isn't revenue yet.

The example

Say you're running a conference in March. Tickets go on sale in October, and by December you've sold 400 seats at $250 each. That's $100,000 in the bank, three months before anyone walks through the door.

Until the event happens, that money is a promise. If the event is postponed or cancelled, most of it goes back to the people who paid. So in the books it sits as deferred revenue, a liability: money you're holding, not money you've earned. Once March comes and you've delivered the event, it becomes revenue.

The same goes for costs you pay early, like the venue deposit and speaker fees. They belong in March too, next to the revenue, so your margin is real.

Where it usually goes wrong

  • December looks like a record month, while March, the month you actually did the work, looks empty.
  • The venue deposit and speaker fees paid early land in the wrong months, so the margin is wrong in both.
  • The ticket money gets spent on the next event before this one has happened. That works fine, until one of them is postponed.

Ticket money is a promise, until the event is done.
