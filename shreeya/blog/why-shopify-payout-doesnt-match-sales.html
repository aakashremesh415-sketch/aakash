Why doesn't your Shopify payout match your sales?

Shopify and Amazon payouts never match sales because fees, refunds and reserves come out first. Five steps, from a clearing account to A2X, make every dollar tie out.

Say your store sold $10,000 this week. The payout that lands in your bank is smaller, and it never seems to line up. That's one of the most common questions I get from eCommerce owners.

The answer: fees, refunds and reserves come out before the payout reaches your bank. The money's all there. It just arrives in pieces. Five levels, and every dollar ties out.

Level 1: Split every payout

A payout isn't one number. Break each one into sales, discounts, refunds, fees and reserves, each on its own line. Once it's split, you can see exactly where the difference went.

Level 2: Use a clearing account

Book sales to a clearing account, not straight to the bank. Each payout then clears that account. Anything left over is a real difference worth looking into, not noise.

Level 3: Automate it with A2X

If you sell on Shopify and Amazon, you don't do this by hand. Connect A2X. It posts one summary per payout, per channel, already mapped to the right accounts.

Level 4: Cost of goods

Book cost of goods in the same month as the sale, so your margin is real. Sales in one month with the product cost in another make both months wrong.

Level 5: Reconcile

At month end, every payout matches a bank deposit, and the clearing account sits at zero. That's how you know it's right.

The short version

The money was never missing. It just arrived in pieces. Split it, automate it, and every month ties out.
