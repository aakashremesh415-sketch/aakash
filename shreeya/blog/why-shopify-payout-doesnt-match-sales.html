Why your Shopify payout doesn't match your sales

Shopify and Amazon payouts never match sales because fees, refunds and reserves come out first. Five steps, from a clearing account to A2X, make every dollar tie out.

Say a store sells $10,000 in a week. The payout that lands in the bank is smaller, and the two never seem to line up. It's one of the most common problems I see in eCommerce books.

The reason: fees, refunds and reserves are taken out before the payout reaches the bank. Nothing is missing. The money simply arrives in pieces. Five steps make every dollar tie out.

Step 1: Split every payout

A payout isn't one number. Break each one into sales, discounts, refunds, fees and reserves, each on its own line. Once it's split, the difference between sales and deposit is fully explained.

Step 2: Use a clearing account

Book sales to a clearing account rather than straight to the bank. Each payout then clears that account. Anything left over is a real difference worth investigating, not noise.

Step 3: Automate with A2X

Splitting payouts by hand doesn't scale, especially across Shopify and Amazon. A2X posts one summary per payout, per channel, already mapped to the right accounts.

Step 4: Match cost of goods to sales

Book cost of goods in the same month as the sale, so the margin is real. Sales in one month with the product cost in another make both months wrong.

Step 5: Reconcile

At month end, every payout should match a bank deposit, and the clearing account should sit at zero. That's the proof the books are right.

In short

Split every payout, automate the posting, and every month ties out.
