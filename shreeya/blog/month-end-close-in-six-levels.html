The month-end close, step by step

Catching up on the books once a year, at tax time, isn't bookkeeping. A proper month-end close has six steps: bank feeds, categorizing, reconciliations, accruals, amortization and P&L analysis.

Many small businesses keep their books once a year: a pile of receipts and statements handed to the accountant in time for the tax return. That isn't bookkeeping. It's a tax-season rush, and it leaves you running the business for eleven months on numbers that are out of date.

A proper month-end close has six steps. Most businesses that catch up once a year are only doing the first one.

Step 1: Bank feeds

Connect every bank account and every card to your accounting system, so no transaction goes missing. An account that isn't connected only shows up when someone remembers to upload a statement, which is usually too late to be useful.

Step 2: Categorize

Code every transaction the same way, every month. Payroll is payroll. Software is software. When the same expense lands in a different account from one month to the next, month-to-month comparisons stop meaning anything.

Step 3: Reconciliations

Reconciliations prove the books are right. Bank accounts, cards and loans must match the books to the cent. Any difference gets investigated and explained before the month is closed, not a year later when nobody remembers what happened.

Step 4: Accruals

Some bills haven't arrived by the time the month ends. Accruals record expenses and revenue in the month they actually happened, not when the invoice shows up. Without them, one month looks better than it was and the next looks worse.

Step 5: Amortization

A year of insurance or software paid upfront is spread across the twelve months it covers. Otherwise one month carries a large expense and the other eleven look cheaper than they really are.

Step 6: P&L analysis

The result of the close: reports ready by day 10 of the following month, compared with the previous month and with budget, with a clear note of what moved and why.

A cycle, not a one-off

Once the six steps are in place, decisions are made on this month's numbers, not last year's. Then the close starts again the next month, in the same order.

A quick checklist:

  • Bank feeds: every account and card connected.
  • Categorize: the same coding every month.
  • Reconciliations: bank, cards and loans match to the cent.
  • Accruals: costs and revenue in the month they happened.
  • Amortization: large upfront payments spread over the months they cover.
  • P&L analysis: reports by day 10, against last month and budget.
